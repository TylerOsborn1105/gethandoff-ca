In Vancouver, you're more likely to be pitched AI than helped with it. Agencies, courses, software demos, all promising a lot and most of them hiding their prices behind a discovery call. If you've sat through one of those and come away more confused, you're not alone.

Handoff is deliberately simple: one person who ran a business himself, who comes to you, teaches first, publishes his prices, and tells you when you don't need him.

## What the free hour looks like here

I come to your studio, shop, clinic or office. You walk me through a normal week and show me the actual tools you use. I teach you one thing you can use the next day, and within a day you get a one-page report with what I'd fix, what I wouldn't bother with, and what each would cost.

## The kind of work I see in Vancouver

Vancouver's small businesses lean toward services, studios, clinics, restaurants and professional offices, where time is the product. The patterns:

- **Booking, rescheduling and reminder messages** that eat into client-facing time.
- **Proposals and quotes** that take longer to write than the work takes to scope.
- **Small teams already using AI, but each person differently**, with no shared approach and no rules about client information.
- **Owners who've tried ChatGPT and found it generic**, usually because it was never set up with anything about their business.

That third one is often the most valuable fix. A half-day where the whole team learns together, on real work, with clear rules about what goes in, turns scattered experiments into a shared habit.
