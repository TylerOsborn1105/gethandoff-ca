**Quotes drafted from your notes**

Whether you call it a quote, an estimate or a proposal, the pattern is the same in most businesses: you talk to the customer, take notes, and then the quote waits until you find a quiet hour. Often that's late at night. Sometimes it's days later, and by then the customer has gone with whoever replied first.

Speed wins work. Here's how to get a quote out the same day without cutting corners.

## The one rule: you own the numbers

Before anything else: **AI writes the words; you set the prices.** It can lay out your pricing, do arithmetic you've told it to do, and write a clear description of the work. It should never invent a price, guess at quantities, or decide on a discount. Tools like ChatGPT will confidently produce numbers that look right and aren't.

Every approach below keeps you in control of the numbers. That's what makes it safe to use for real work.

## Step 1: Build a quote kit (once)

Spend an hour collecting what AI needs to write quotes that sound like you:

- **Three to five good past quotes**, the ones you were proudest of, with customer details removed.
- **Your price list or rates**, even if it's rough: hourly rates, common items, minimum charges.
- **Your standard terms**: deposit, payment terms, what's included and excluded, how long the quote is valid.
- **A few notes on style**: "short and plain," "always list what's not included," "never promise a finish date."

Save this in one document. If you use ChatGPT, you can put it in a Project (or a custom GPT) so it's there every time without pasting. Copilot and Gemini can read it from a file in your OneDrive or Google Drive.

## Step 2: Capture notes while they're fresh

The best time to start a quote is right after the conversation, not at night. Record a two-minute voice memo on your phone before you drive away or hang up:

> "Quote for the office on Elm. They want the two meeting rooms done, not the kitchen. Timeline is before the end of the month. Mentioned budget is tight, so give them the standard option and the premium as an extra. Need to check the parking situation."

Most phones will turn that into text. That transcript is all AI needs.

## Step 3: Turn notes into a draft

Paste the transcript in with an instruction like this:

```
Using my quote kit, draft a quote from these notes.
Use only prices from my price list. If something isn't on the list,
leave a blank marked [PRICE NEEDED] instead of guessing.
List what's included, what's not included, and any questions I still need to answer.
Notes: [paste transcript]
```

Two parts of that instruction do most of the work. "Only prices from my price list" stops it making numbers up. "Questions I still need to answer" catches the gaps you'd otherwise discover after sending.

## Step 4: Check it like it's your name on it

Because it is. Go through it in this order:

1. **Every number.** Quantities, rates, totals, tax. Recalculate the total yourself or in a spreadsheet. Don't trust AI's arithmetic on the final figure.
2. **Scope.** Is everything they asked for there? Is anything there they didn't ask for?
3. **Promises.** Dates, guarantees, "includes" statements. AI tends to be generous with promises.
4. **Tone.** Does it sound like you? Add one line only you would write.

With practice, this check takes five to ten minutes, compared with 30 to 60 minutes of writing from scratch.

## Step 5: Send it, then follow up

A quote sent the same day beats a better quote sent next week. Once it's out, set a reminder to follow up in three to five days. AI can draft that too:

```
Draft a short, friendly follow-up for a quote I sent on Monday.
Don't be pushy. Ask if they have any questions and mention we can start [date].
```

A surprising amount of work is won in the follow-up, simply because most businesses never send one.

## When to set it up properly

The steps above work with nothing more than a phone and a chat tool. Once you're doing it regularly, it's worth setting it up so it runs with fewer steps: your voice memo goes in, and a draft appears in your quoting software or inbox, formatted and ready to check. That's usually a small setup job, not a big build.
