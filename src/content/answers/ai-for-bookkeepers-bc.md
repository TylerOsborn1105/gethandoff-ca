The receipts are still outstanding. The same client questions came in again this week. The follow-up you meant to send is still a draft. That pile is the useful part of AI for bookkeepers in BC. A chat tool can draft the wording. It does not keep the books, and it does not need the client's numbers to write a clear note.

**The short answer:** use the tool for the message, from facts you already have. Keep names, amounts, and account numbers out. Read every line before a client sees it.

*This is practical guidance, not legal advice. I checked the Act and the commissioner's guide on 30 October 2026. A lawyer is the right person for a decision about a specific client file.*

## What piles up at month-end?

The books wait on other people, and on messages you have already written in your head.

- Clients who have not sent the receipts, the bills, or the statements you asked for last week.
- The same questions, every week: what you still need, when it is due, and what happens if it arrives late.
- Follow-up emails that are ready in your notes and never leave the drafts folder.
- A short status note for the client who asks, again, where the file stands.

None of that is the judgment in the file. It is the writing around the judgment. The writing is what sits until the evening.

## What can a chat tool draft?

A chat tool is a reasonable way to get a first draft of those messages. You already know what is missing and what you tell people. The tool does not.

It can draft:

- A receipt chase that says what is missing, in plain words, without scolding.
- A reply to a question you answer every week, in the same short style you already use.
- A follow-up that was never sent, from the point you meant to make.
- A status note: what you have, what you are still waiting on, and what you need next. No figures.

Paste the shape of the message, not the file. "The client has not sent three expense receipts from last month. Ask again, politely, and say which kinds of receipts are still missing." That is enough. Put the real name, the dollar amounts, and the dates back in yourself, on your own computer, after you read the draft.

If a line states a deadline, a penalty, or a tax result you did not provide, delete it. The tool will fill gaps so the note sounds finished. Those gaps are yours to fill, or to leave blank.

## What client financial information stays out?

The Office of the Information and Privacy Commissioner for BC says personal information is information about an identifiable individual. A name is the obvious case. So is a detail that identifies someone when it sits beside other details. The guide's example is a health report with no name that still identifies the person through an address, an ID number, or a phone number. A tax page can do the same.

Keep these out of the tool:

- Social insurance numbers.
- Bank account numbers, card numbers, and passwords.
- A full return, a full set of statements, or a payroll list with names and pay.
- Home address, date of birth, and what the person earns, spends, or owes.
- Notes the client expected you to keep inside the file.

A client's question, with the name and the numbers taken out, is enough for a draft. "The customer wants to know which receipts are still missing" works. The spreadsheet does not.

If you are not certain the tool keeps what you type to your work account, do not test that theory with a client file. Leave the details out.

## What does PIPA expect of a bookkeeper?

PIPA is the Personal Information Protection Act, SBC 2003, c. 63. The copy on BC Laws was current to 27 October 2026. Section 3 says it applies to every organization. The commissioner's guide for businesses (October 2015, 5th publication) says that includes an individual carrying on a commercial activity.

Personal information means information about an identifiable individual. The guide's example is an accounting firm that receives names, social insurance numbers, hours, and rates of pay. Payroll from a private-sector client is covered by PIPA. Payroll a municipality still controls can fall under the public-sector law instead.

Section 11 limits collection to purposes a reasonable person would consider appropriate, and to what you disclosed. Section 14 limits use the same way. Section 34 requires reasonable security arrangements against unauthorized access, use, disclosure, and similar risks. A chat tool is a new use. If the client was not told, leave the file out.
